# Garden Journal — Product Vision

## Core idea

Your words slowly become a garden.

Garden Journal is a calm journaling experience where writing itself has value.
The garden grows as a visual record of reflection over time.

The product is built around three layers.

## 1. Daily Loop

Once per day:

Write → Receive one drop → Plant or grow something

The goal is to create a small, gentle reason to return tomorrow.

Principles:

- One meaningful growth opportunity per day
- Writing itself earns the reward
- Negative emotions are not punished
- No XP, ranking, or aggressive streak mechanics
- Interaction should remain lightweight

## 2. Emergent Garden

The garden should gradually become unpredictable and personal.

Future possibilities:

- Flower colors influenced by nearby flowers
- Probabilistic mutations
- Different growth depending on surrounding plants
- Rare variations
- Small environmental changes
- Gardens naturally becoming different between users

The goal is not optimization.

The user should sometimes think:

"I wonder what will grow tomorrow."

## 3. Long-term Reflection

AI should help users understand what they have been thinking about over time.

Examples:

- Weekly reflection
- Monthly reflection
- Recurring themes
- Changes in interests or concerns
- Things the user repeatedly returned to

Example:

"This month, you often came back to work, learning, and change."

AI should summarize and reflect, not score or diagnose the user.

These long-term patterns may also gently influence the garden.

Examples:

- recurring themes affect plant varieties
- reflection influences garden atmosphere
- monthly patterns unlock subtle environmental changes

The relationship should be:

Journal → Reflection → Garden

not:

Journal → AI score → Reward

## Product philosophy

Private journal.
Living garden.
Quiet shared space.

The journal is primarily private.
The garden may be shared.

The product should encourage curiosity and reflection rather than competition.

Avoid:

- rankings
- follower counts as a core mechanic
- pressure-heavy streaks
- positivity scores
- journal quality scores
- gamification that rewards writing more often than reflecting

## Long-term experience

Daily:
"What will grow today?"

Weekly:
"What have I been writing about?"

Monthly:
"How has my garden — and my thinking — changed?"

Over time:
"This garden could only have become mine."
